# Bibliography audit — 2026-09-13

## Scope and conclusion

This audit covers all **21 entries present in `paper/refs.bib` when the audit began**.
It verifies reference identity, title, listed authors, publication year/status, venue,
identifiers, and relevant version history against primary records. It also checks how
the manuscript uses those references. New-literature selection is a separate task.

All 21 references identify real sources. The principal problem was **stale publication
metadata**, rather than invented papers. Seven entries described as arXiv preprints
have verified proceedings publications: Mathews/Nagappan, LiveCodeBench, Ahmed et al.,
CodeJudgeBench, TDFlow, Jin/Chen, and ConVerTest. The resampling paper also has an ICLR
2026 proceedings publication. TENET's current arXiv record reports ISSRE 2026 acceptance;
this audit does not claim to have verified its final proceedings record.

Existing cite keys are retained, including keys containing a superseded year or the
historical `sun2025tenet` author mnemonic. A cite key is an internal identifier, not
evidence of authorship or date. No new bibliography entries were added by this audit.

## Evidence standard and access limits

- Prefer the publisher/proceedings record over an older arXiv record for published
  metadata. ArXiv is authoritative for its own versions, posting dates, and authors'
  acceptance notices, but an absent journal-reference field does not establish that
  a paper remains unpublished.
- Publisher-deposited Crossref records were retrieved directly where ACM/IEEE pages
  were inaccessible to the browser tool. These are bibliographic records deposited by
  the publisher, not independent replications or evaluations of the research.
- OpenReview returned browser challenges for several known records. Official ICLR
  proceedings pages and the official ICLR 2023 event page provided independent primary
  confirmation of publication and identity. The stable OpenReview identifiers remain
  useful bibliography links.
- Fabio Pereira's original blog was retrieved and read through `curl`; the browser tool
  failed to open the same URL. That browser failure is not evidence of a dead source.
- No withdrawal, retraction, or correction notice was visible in the primary records
  inspected. The inspected Crossref records did not expose a retraction/update notice.
  This is a bounded check of those records, not a guarantee that no notice exists in
  any index or venue. Version and title changes identified below are ordinary updates.
- Long author lists intentionally abbreviated with `and others` remain abbreviated.
  Authors named explicitly in the other entries were checked in order. Where a primary
  landing page and its PDF differed in name detail, the published PDF was preferred.

## Citation-to-claim observations for manuscript authors

These observations refer to the manuscript read at the start of this audit; parallel
manuscript editing may resolve them. This audit changes bibliography metadata only.

1. **The original HumanEval paper is missing from rendered citations.**
   `chen2021humaneval` exists in the bibliography but had no `\cite` occurrence in
   `main.tex`. Cite it at the first substantive definition of HumanEval. EvalPlus should
   still accompany claims about the expanded HumanEval+ suites. The original benchmark
   and its test augmentation are different contributions. [HumanEval source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374v2)

2. **Keep the endpoints distinct in the code-judge paragraph.** Jin and Chen's study
   specifically exposes correct implementations being rejected under natural-language
   checking, with prompting effects. The manuscript's broad phrase “consistently
   struggle” is less precise than stating that measured failure mode. It should not
   be treated as direct evidence of this paper's false-accept rate. [Source abstract and paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12358)

3. **The Sonnet 4.5 caveat remains necessary after upgrading CodeJudgeBench's citation.**
   The final ACL paper's model descriptions still name Claude-3.5-Sonnet-v2,
   Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Claude-4-Sonnet, and Claude-4-Opus, with Gemini-2.5-Pro/Flash.
   Its published status does not make its results evidence about Claude Sonnet 4.5.
   The manuscript's explicit distinction is accurate. Its final paper also adds
   robustness experiments, so future quantitative quotations should identify the
   final-paper table rather than assume the 2025 arXiv table is unchanged.
   [Final CodeJudgeBench paper, model details](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.888.pdf)

4. **TENET and TDFlow use supplied tests in their central evaluations.** The manuscript
   already contrasts externally supplied/human-checked tests with autonomous authorship,
   but “extend test-driven loops to repository-scale agents” alone can blur this
   distinction. TENET uses developer-written tests to guide repository-context function
   generation. TDFlow's headline results are explicitly conditioned on human-written
   tests, with test generation a separate component/analysis. Neither headline result
   establishes calibration of a fully self-authored acceptance loop.
   [TENET v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24148v4),
   [TDFlow publication](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.70/)

5. **TiCoder's scaled evaluation is not a large human-user trial.** The source includes
   a 15-programmer user study and a separate larger evaluation using idealized proxy
   feedback. The manuscript's qualitative description of user intent clarification is
   supported. Any new numerical claim about its improvement should state which
   experiment supplied it. [TiCoder journal version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10100v2)

6. **Stroebl et al. analyze a related but different control flow.** Resampling against
   imperfect verifiers supports discussing conditional correctness and selection
   limits. It is not a proof that a stronger natural-language reviewer cannot improve
   a TDD pipeline, nor an experiment randomizing this manuscript's retry feedback.
   The revised manuscript's bounded connection is appropriate.
   [ICLR 2026 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/95591b7557edea46ae1d90b0e9e825c5-Abstract-Conference.html)

7. **Practitioner sources establish terminology, not experimental prevalence.** Rudolph
   and Pereira support the descriptions of implementation-mirroring/tautological tests.
   Their blog posts do not measure prevalence in LLM agents. Beck establishes the TDD
   practice; its publication metadata alone is not causal evidence that TDD improves
   any given modern pipeline.
   [Rudolph](https://jasonrudolph.com/blog/2008/07/30/testing-anti-patterns-the-ugly-mirror/),
   [Pereira](https://fabiopereira.me/blog/2010/05/27/ttdd-tautological-test-driven-development-anti-pattern/),
   [Beck publisher record](https://www.informit.com/store/test-driven-development-by-example-9780321146533)

8. **The self-citation's scope is accurately limited.** The context-ablation paper holds
   localization fixed and varies representation, with independent SWE-bench outcomes
   and repeated runs. It supports methodological context and does not replicate this
   paper's TDD interventions. Its arXiv posting should not be described as evidence of
   peer review. [Context paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09691)

The broad factual uses of EvalPlus, LiveCodeBench, CodeT, ConVerTest, MetaGPT, Barr et al.,
and Boehm match the primary sources inspected. The current distinction between the
observational RGRBench retry grouping and Ahmed et al.'s refinement intervention should
be retained. No metadata correction changes the manuscript's recorded experimental data.
